# Fred Again.. Production Style & Technique Research
### Compiled for a Producer's Practice Workbook

**Subject:** Fred John Philip Gibson ("Fred again..")
**Purpose:** Technical, source-cited breakdown of arrangement, drum treatment, bass movement, vocal sampling, transitions, and emotional pacing across his most-analyzed tracks, plus his stated workflow, gear, and habits.

**Tracks covered:** Delilah (pull me out of this); Marea (we've lost dancing); Danielle (smile on my face); Turn On The Lights again.. (feat. Future, w/ Swedish House Mafia); Rumble (w/ Skrillex & Flowdan); Adore U (w/ Obongjayar); Kammy (like i do) / "Kammy's Song"; Places to be (w/ Anderson .Paak); Baby again.. (w/ Skrillex); plus supporting references to Ten, Marnie (Wish I Had U), USB-era tracks (Lightsburn Dimmer, Solo), and Winny.

> Note on sourcing: Fred again.. does not release official stems or plugin-chain documentation. Most granular technical specs below come from ear-trained reverse-engineering / remake tutorials (Ableton/Logic/Serum breakdowns), mix-analysis writeups, and Attack Magazine's "Beat Dissected" series, cross-checked against his own interview statements (Tape Notes, Sound on Sound, Zane Lowe/Apple Music, MusicRadar). Reverse-engineered specs are exact numbers *as demonstrated by the tutorial creator's ear*, not confirmed session data — flagged inline as "(remake estimate)" where relevant. Direct Fred quotes and confirmed credits are marked as such.

## 0. WORKFLOW, GEAR & STATED PROCESS (from his own interviews)

### DAW and core setup
- Despite many fan-made tutorials using Ableton Live (because it's easier to demonstrate publicly), Fred's **stated favorite/primary DAW is Logic Pro** — confirmed by Touch Loops ("We know Fred Again's favourite DAW is Logic Pro — We're just showing you it's possible, even if you don't have the same equipment or DAW as Fred Again") ([Touch Loops / Creating The Sounds Of Fred Again..](https://touchloops.com/blogs/news/how-to-sound-like-fred-again)) and by Gearnews' live-setup writeup, which states "Apple Logic Pro (or Ableton Live) sits at the center of his setup" and that the Tape Notes episode on *USB* included "screen capture from Fred's logic sessions" ([Gearnews, Fred again.. Live Setup](https://www.gearnews.com/fred-again-gear-sound-synths/); [Tape Notes TN:176 transcript](https://podcasttranscript.ai/library/tn-176-fred-again)).
- Live shows and studio sketching also lean on **Native Instruments Maschine / Maschine+** (a modern 16-pad MPC-style sampler) and an **Akai MPC-style workflow** — demonstrated live to Zane Lowe, where Fred said of the Maschine: "You can play anything on it, like, it's so infinitely powerful... with electronic music, it's often almost impossible to get it 'live *live*.'" ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/fred-again-zane-lowe-native-instruments-maschine-live-set/); [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/fred-again-wows-zane-lowe-mpc-new-interview-1234618577/); [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/fred-again-maschine-brian-eno)).
- Live performances are described as full reconstructions, not simple playback: tracks are broken apart and rebuilt on stage using clips running in Logic/Ableton triggered live, with Ableton Push and Maschine handling drums, melodies, and vocal slices ([Gearnews](https://www.gearnews.com/fred-again-gear-sound-synths/)).

### Recording philosophy — the iPhone is central
- **"I record everything on my iPhone, or everything apart from lead vocals... I record guitars, I record all the backing vocals, I record drums. I love being able to just capture something and then chuck it into my laptop and warp or manipulate it and turn it into something totally different."** ([Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/fred-gibson-aka-fred-again?page=2))
- He explicitly prefers the **iPhone's built-in microphone and compression** over pro mics for many uses: *"It's the in-built compression of the iPhone I adore... whenever I use an SM7, I have a plug-in chain I use to try and recreate the compression of the iPhone."* He still uses a **Shure SM7B** for pop/rap lead vocal sessions (e.g., with Stormzy, Ed Sheeran), calling it "the best mic ever," and sometimes uses **Neumann** mics ([Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/fred-gibson-aka-fred-again?page=2)).
- Vocal recording ritual for his own voice: he avoids formal booths, goes to an "outside space," and **sings into his iPhone's Voice Memos app**, sometimes sending the take to a friend over **WhatsApp** because he loves the audio compression WhatsApp/voice-notes applies to the file ([Underdog Music School breakdown of Tape Notes comments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ByjKEl_uO9g); [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/fred-gibson-aka-fred-again?page=2)). Typical method: play the song, pause it, sing an idea a cappella into the phone (still in key from having just heard it), AirDrop it to his laptop, and drag it into the session — "it usually lines up."
- On his phone he uses **Koala** (by elf audio) — an MPC-style sampler app — to "record random bits and manipulate them in the phone, then record them back into the computer," which he calls "so expressive as instruments... you can really quickly get really cool results" ([Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/fred-gibson-aka-fred-again?page=2)). He has also been shown demoing **Tape It**, an iPhone voice-memo/field-recording app for musicians, on the Tape Notes podcast ([Tape Notes TN:176](https://podcasttranscript.ai/library/tn-176-fred-again)).
- Source material for vocals/samples is deliberately non-studio: Instagram videos, voice memos, phone conversations, spoken-word/open-mic clips, field recordings (London Underground ambience, birdsong in North-West London), and chance encounters (e.g., a construction worker named Carlos recorded in an Atlanta bar became a sample source) ([Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/fred-gibson-aka-fred-again?page=2); [Scribd case study](https://www.scribd.com/document/959085711/Riad-Connor-Elbaba-Production-Styles-Written-Case-Study)).

### Vocal processing chain philosophy
- Signature move: **put reverb before compression**, not after. *"Aside from quite extreme compression, I love putting the reverb before the compressor. You can do it with a massive reverb or with a really small room reverb and it means you still get that clarity and closeness."* This exaggerates the iPhone mic's natural behavior — close/dry while speaking, then the room "fills up the gap" with reverb the instant the voice stops — so processing this way keeps intimacy on the voice while still getting an emotionally spacious tail ([Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/fred-gibson-aka-fred-again?page=2)).
- He treats studio "rules" as flexible: rules built for a clean-source scenario (e.g., a string quartet at Abbey Road, or the D'Angelo *Voodoo* drum sound) don't apply when the source is a YouTube rip or lo-fi phone recording — in that case he leans into de-noising/de-harshing plugins rather than a "correct" signal chain, citing **Skrillex** as a kindred spirit who works with whatever source material he has and applies "intentionally reckless" sound design ([Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/fred-gibson-aka-fred-again?page=2)).

### Plugin minimalism (stated directly by Fred)
- On the **Tape Notes** podcast, Fred explicitly tells producers to stop plugin-shopping: *"I've spent so many thousands of hours wasted on plugins, getting into the weeds with these things... in terms of your reverb, compressor, EQ, all these things, I would just say, delete everything apart from… choose one, two, three, four, five, it doesn't matter what you choose. They're all great. Choose which one you like the sound of most and just work within that, and then focus on writing songs."* ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/ive-spent-so-many-thousands-of-hours-wasted-on-plugins-it-just-doesnt-matter-fred-again-on-how-getting-into-the-weeds-with-software-can-distract-from-songwriting-and-make-you-less-creative))
- Favorite **Logic** stock plugins named directly: the legacy **Silver Compressor** (hidden in Logic's legacy-plugin menu, accessed by holding Alt) — a simple two-dial compressor — and the legacy **AVerb** reverb, which he describes as having a "hollow longing" quality; Logic's newer **ChromaVerb** is "good" but serves a different purpose to AVerb, in his view ([Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/fred-gibson-aka-fred-again?page=2)).
- Reported workflow anecdote: while mastering Eno/Hyde's *High Life* (2014), the desired mix only existed as an MP3; Fred dragged the MP3 into Logic and bounced it to WAV to send for mastering — illustrating a loose, "whatever gets it done" studio ethos he carries into his own music ([Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/fred-gibson-aka-fred-again?page=2)).

### Daily practice / creative habit
- **"I try and make a few ideas a day, and most of them are rubbish… The best feeling to me is when you play back the thing you've made that day and you actually feel good about it. That's the drug I'm chasing every day."** ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/the-best-feeling-is-when-you-play-back-the-thing-youve-made-that-day-and-you-actually-feel-good-about-it-thats-the-drug-im-chasing-every-day-unlocking-the-production-tricks-that-fred-again-swears-by))
- He treats the creative process as playful trial-and-error: *"the joy and the journey of finding out what you like, just like closing your eyes and throwing paint and seeing what sticks."* His mentor **Brian Eno** reinforced this — encouraging play in the studio, exploring unfamiliar sonic territory, and prioritizing joy/curiosity over technical polish ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/the-best-feeling-is-when-you-play-back-the-thing-youve-made-that-day-and-you-actually-feel-good-about-it-thats-the-drug-im-chasing-every-day-unlocking-the-production-tricks-that-fred-again-swears-by); [Scribd case study](https://www.scribd.com/document/959085711/Riad-Connor-Elbaba-Production-Styles-Written-Case-Study)).
- Tape Notes episodes (TN:75, TN:105, TN:176) repeatedly document: writing/producing very fast (put off "technical" decisions until the very end of a session); recreating the "voice notes compressor" from scratch inside Logic; jamming along to tracks in a club to test them; doing informal "playback sessions" in a pub to gauge reactions; and his belief that *"mixing will never solve any song problems"* — i.e., song and arrangement decisions come first, sonic polish later ([Tape Notes TN:75](https://soundcloud.com/user-842890129/tn-75-fred-again); [Tape Notes TN:105](https://tapenotes.co.uk/project/tn105-fred-again)).
- On the **USB** project (2024–25), he describes it as an "infinite, evolving mixtape" free of album-narrative constraints, built via granular, exploratory sampling: extracting a vocal from Jamie T for "Lightsburn Dimmer," building "Solo" from a sample by visual artist Splinter Frames, and using **Melodyne** heavily to manipulate polyphonic audio, shift harmonies, and try different keys — describing the process as trying many combinations of vocal/percussion fragments through trial and error "until elements harmoniously 'lock'" ([Tape Notes TN:176 transcript](https://podcasttranscript.ai/library/tn-176-fred-again)).
- On sound-design attitude for melody: he'll often build a "drone" (a single sustained note/chord run through a long reverb) as a harmonic bed before anything else, then improvise a vocal or melody over it — described as a technique borrowed from ambient music and Eno's influence ("Fred's Production Secrets" breakdown, [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xwUTqc6gUQE)).

### Live show design
- His famous viral **Boiler Room**-style live sets are not DJ sets of finished tracks — they are live reconstructions: samples/loops/beats are triggered, modified and recombined in real time using Maschine/Push/Logic, so every performance of a "song" is different, and buildups/breaks are extended or shortened based on crowd energy ([Gearnews](https://www.gearnews.com/fred-again-gear-sound-synths/); [Decoded Magazine on the Zane Lowe interview](https://www.decodedmagazine.com/fred-again-speaks-to-zane-lowe-about-actual-life-3-his-relationship-with-brian-eno-ed-sheeran-burna-boy-and-that-boiler-room-set/)).
- The large screen behind him on stage displays footage/clips from the phones of the people he sampled — he calls it "part of the DNA of the music," directly tying the visual show to the sampling-based writing process ([Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/fred-again-wows-zane-lowe-mpc-new-interview-1234618577/)).
- Demoed live for Zane Lowe: rebuilding "I Found You" from scratch on stage, starting from layered drones (a technique developed with Eno, who Fred says they'd spend "hours" just listening to how a single drone evolves) before layering in re-pitched spoken-word samples ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/fred-again-zane-lowe-native-instruments-maschine-live-set/)).

## 1. ARRANGEMENT

### General patterns across his catalogue
- **Loop-based, not strictly linear**: most tracks are built from a small number of 4–16 bar loops that are muted/unmuted, filtered in/out, and re-combined rather than a long sequence of unique sections — consistent with a Maschine/Push-style live-performance workflow bleeding into studio arrangement ([Gearnews](https://www.gearnews.com/fred-again-gear-sound-synths/)).
- **Three-act shape**: intro (vocal/emotional statement, minimal beat) → building middle section (layers accumulate, energy climbs) → drop/release (full arrangement, hook repeats) is identified as his most common macro-structure, paired with deliberately simple chord counts (fewer changes than typical pop) compensated for by dense atmospheric/vocal-sample layering ([Surge Sounds, "What Genre is Fred Again?"](https://surgesounds.com/post/what-genre-is-fred-again-recreating-his-signature-sound)).
- **Short/abrupt or "shortest intro ever"** is called out as a recognizable trait in breakdown videos — tracks like "Delilah" get into the loop and hook extremely fast relative to typical house/pop convention ([Underdog Music School, "The Fred Again.. phenomenon (5 lessons)"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ByjKEl_uO9g)).
- **Muting/filtering as arrangement tool, not just effect**: rather than writing entirely new sections, Fred frequently mutes a subset of elements (bass, hats, or the main hook) at a transition point to create a "new" section from the same material — see "dropstep" technique below (Transitions section).
- **Genre range dictates arrangement density**: darker minimal-garage tracks ("Kammy") strip down to two-step drums + Reese bass + chopped vocals; euphoric tracks ("Delilah") are dense, punchy, with repetitive vocal loops; slow, intimate tracks ("Mustafa," "Berwyn") drop tempo/energy almost entirely and foreground a single vocal and piano/pad texture ([Scribd Actual Life 3 case study](https://www.scribd.com/document/959085711/Riad-Connor-Elbaba-Production-Styles-Written-Case-Study)).

### Delilah (pull me out of this)
- Structure demonstrated in a full remake: **drum samples → swing/groove pool → drum pattern → drum effects → piano chords → piano effects → bassline → bass sound design → bass effects → lead melody → lead sound design/effects → synth pad → pad sound design/effects → secondary melody (doubled an octave) → vocal → additional kick → outro**, i.e., the song is essentially five interlocking loop-based layers (drums, piano, bass, lead, pad) plus a vocal sample, layered and subtracted across the arrangement rather than written as unique sections ([Jaron Lopez remake, YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4wkqzqMEYfQ)).
- Track was reportedly built extremely fast: Songfacts states Fred created the core of "Delilah" in **roughly 45 minutes**, part of his stated "spontaneous inspiration" workflow ([Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/fred-again/delilah-pull-me-out-of-this)).
- The drum pattern "briefly takes respite midway through before restoring the beat" — a stripped-back mid-track breakdown that returns to full arrangement, described as mirroring the "thumping heart" anxiety narrative of the lyric ([The Indiependent track review](https://www.indiependent.co.uk/track-review-delilah-pull-me-out-of-this-fred-again/)).
- An **occasional extra kick placed between beats one and two** recurs throughout the arrangement purely to add periodic energy lift without otherwise altering the groove — a "micro-arrangement" device rather than a full section change (Jaron Lopez remake, timestamp ~22:41, [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4wkqzqMEYfQ)).
- Credits show a wide production team shaping structure: Skrillex is credited for "additional production," specifically **shaping drums and structure**; Kieran Hebden (Four Tet) and Alex Gibson (Fred's brother) contributed programming "to build its dynamic structure" ([Scribd case study](https://www.scribd.com/document/959085711/Riad-Connor-Elbaba-Production-Styles-Written-Case-Study); [Qobuz credits via Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/skrillex/comments/y6ix5m/fred_again_delilah_pull_me_out_of_this_coprod/)).

### Marea (we've lost dancing)
- 4/4, ~125 BPM, steady four-on-the-floor foundation; **minimalistic percussion** (kick, hats, claps) leaves maximal space for the sampled vocal and pads to carry the arrangement ([Jai Bhayani analysis](https://www.jaibhayani.com/post/how-we-ve-lost-dancing-ironically-got-the-world-dancing)).
- Notable arrangement feature: an unusual, **stripped-back breakdown mid-track** that foregrounds just the vocal sample and pads, described as "a moment of introspection before the energy builds back up" — an atypical placement for a breakdown in club music, timed to match the emotional content of the sampled conversation ([Jai Bhayani analysis](https://www.jaibhayani.com/post/how-we-ve-lost-dancing-ironically-got-the-world-dancing)).
- The track's lyric is **unwritten** — entirely composed of a sampled Zoom conversation with The Blessed Madonna, meaning the "verse structure" is dictated by the natural cadence and pauses of real speech rather than conventional songwriting, then arranged/tuned into a singing melody ([Genius](https://genius.com/Fred-again-marea-weve-lost-dancing-lyrics); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marea_(We've_Lost_Dancing))).

### Danielle (smile on my face)
- Co-produced with Four Tet, contributing "ambient textures and intricate rhythms" per the case study; built around 070 Shake's vocal and material from her song "Nice to Have" ([Scribd case study](https://www.scribd.com/document/959085711/Riad-Connor-Elbaba-Production-Styles-Written-Case-Study)).
- Bass part is a defining structural motif: a rolling triplet-grid bassline at **130 BPM** repeats across most of the track with minor pitch-register shifts between sections (see Bass section below) — i.e., arrangement variety is created by transposing the same rhythmic bass figure rather than writing a new bassline per section (Alux Serum bass tutorial, [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JPFaOFmZxYU)).

### Rumble (Skrillex, Flowdan, Fred again..)
- Structurally the most maximalist/dubstep-adjacent track in the set: built from **layered bell/atmosphere textures (Omnisphere), a wave-folded Serum wobble bass, a rimshot/snare bus, and two separately designed kicks (a main kick and a short 16th-note kick)** combined into a dense low-end/percussion wall — a departure from the sparser house/garage arrangement approach of his solo material, reflecting Skrillex's dubstep/maximalist arrangement influence ([Freddie Fairweather remake breakdown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OOrnRYULQVM)).
- The track won the **Grammy for Best Dance/Electronic Recording**, and *Actual Life 3* won **Best Dance/Electronic Album** — noted alongside a description of his broader "intimate but anthemic" arrangement approach ([Surge Sounds](https://surgesounds.com/post/what-genre-is-fred-again-recreating-his-signature-sound)).

### Adore U (with Obongjayar)
- Samples Obongjayar's own ballad "I Wish It Was Me" (from *Some Nights I Dream of Doors*), retelling it with a beat — described as "increasingly warm and euphoric house music" building under "chilling vocals," eventually backed by "a prominent hip-hop sample that seamlessly integrates without disrupting the vibrant atmosphere" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adore_U); [EDM Identity](https://edmidentity.com/2023/08/14/fred-again-abongjayar-adore-u/)).
- Arrangement is explicitly **non-traditional in energy flow**: a user review notes "the way the beat comes in and out of the song isn't traditional by any means but works perfectly for the vibe of the track," and EDM Identity notes tension "turns up ever so slightly at the bridge three quarters of the way through the song's runtime before resolving to the tranquil harmonics that introduced the concept" — i.e., a late, subtle tension bridge rather than a single big mid-track drop ([Album of the Year user reviews](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/723399-fred-again-adore-u/user-reviews/); [EDM Identity](https://edmidentity.com/2023/08/14/fred-again-abongjayar-adore-u/)).
- A secondary "funky sample" is introduced **around the two-minute mark**, adding a fresh burst of energy/playfulness partway through rather than at a conventional drop point ([Stay Free Radio review](https://www.stayfreeradioip.com/post/fred-again-adore-u-featuring-obongjayar-meaning-and-review)).

### Kammy (like i do) / "Kammy's Song"
- Attack Magazine's official "Beat Dissected" tutorial treats the beat as a **four-bar MIDI loop** built up element by element (kick → snare → 909 hats → shakers → bus processing) — the clearest documented example of his loop-first, layer-by-layer construction method ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/breaking-down-how-to-make-beats-inspired-by-fred-again/)).
- Described in the Actual Life 3 case study as "a darker, minimal-garage approach with two-step drums, a brooding Reese bassline, and club-style chopped vocals" — grittier/more atmospheric than "Delilah" ([Scribd case study](https://www.scribd.com/document/959085711/Riad-Connor-Elbaba-Production-Styles-Written-Case-Study)).

### Places to be (feat. Anderson .Paak)
- Pad/lead breakdown shows chord-driven arrangement with automated pitch bends at phrase ends and arpeggiated rhythmic movement layered under Anderson .Paak's vocal, indicating the synth arrangement itself carries rhythmic interest independent of the drum pattern ([Producer remake breakdown, YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XT7FZAQk-tA)).

### Turn On The Lights again.. (feat. Future, with Swedish House Mafia)
- Built as a **remix/interpolation of Future's 2012 "Turn On the Lights"**: Fred and Swedish House Mafia effectively re-produce the beat and vocal treatment around Future's existing top line ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_On_the_Lights_(song))).
- Remake breakdowns describe the arrangement as clearly divided into **three structural drum layers** (kick+snare, hi-hat pattern, sampled "top loop" for room/vintage texture) that combine and separate to build/release the arrangement, plus a distinct **bass "drop" moment early in the song** using a sampled bass hit transposed and layered with a Serum-designed wobble bass for the escalation ([Sonic Academy drum breakdown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q-iV1msL2xI); [remake tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGb7mVJd0jU)). Track runs 4:27 and charted UK #27 / Ireland #23 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_On_the_Lights_(song))).

### Baby again.. (with Skrillex)
- Referenced in sound-design breakdown videos primarily for its distinctive "cookie drop" bass patch (mono Serum patch with tightly tuned envelopes) — indicates a maximalist, Skrillex-style drop-arrangement approach similar to "Rumble" ([13 Fred Again Sounds deconstruction](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=irMBByLQO9I)).

## 2. DRUM TREATMENT

### Kick design
- Signature kick approach on "Delilah": a **layered stock-sample kick ("Bricks")** with aggressive filtering (low cut at 35 Hz, high cut at 15 kHz) plus targeted midrange EQ moves (cut 150 Hz, boost 1.5 kHz, cut 6 kHz, boost 480 Hz), then run through **soft clipping (threshold −12 dB, +6 dB makeup)** and Ableton's **Drum Buss** (Crunch 20%, Damp 20 kHz, Transients −0.25, Dry/Wet 30%) for glue and character (remake estimate) ([Jaron Lopez remake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4wkqzqMEYfQ)).
- On "Kammy," the kick programming is explicitly called "slightly unorthodox" — using a 909 kick sample ("Kick 909 Tune 4," airy attack, full tail) but **reducing velocity on the first hit whenever two consecutive 16th-note kicks occur**, avoiding a robotic, evenly-loud kick pattern ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/breaking-down-how-to-make-beats-inspired-by-fred-again/)).
- Deconstruction videos consistently note Fred **layering two kick sounds**: a "deeper" main kick plus a separate transient-focused kick, bussed together and EQ'd (subtractive low-end tidy-up, then a bus EQ boosting highs/presence and scooping mids) ([How To Fred Again.. tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EmysxwjOZPA)).
- On "Rumble," two purpose-built kicks are used: a **very short 16th-note kick** and a **main kick**, both shaped in **Kick 2** by matching envelope/pitch curves to the original Skrillex kick reference, then bussed with Decapitator (saturation), a clip stage (OVC), and limiting (Pro-L2) ([Freddie Fairweather remake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OOrnRYULQVM)).
- On "Turn On The Lights again..," the kick is a "murder beats" pack sample duplicated into two layers — one with low end cut, heavy compression and added room noise for size and texture ([remake tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGb7mVJd0jU)).

### Snare/clap and hat design
- "Delilah" uses layered snare/clap material ("Snare Vinyl DLA4" pitched down 2 semitones, "Clap Streets" also pitched down and heavily filtered), plus a secondary "Snare Clapper" pitched up, all individually band-pass filtered around 800 Hz–4 kHz to carve distinct spectral pockets so the layers don't mask each other (remake estimate) ([Jaron Lopez remake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4wkqzqMEYfQ)).
- Recurring technique across multiple breakdowns: **sampling snares directly from other tracks** he admires — e.g., a snare "reminded of" Kanye West's *Good Morning*/"Famous" was sampled and layered with a rimshot for "Turn On The Lights again.." ([remake tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGb7mVJd0jU); ["13 Fred Again Sounds"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=irMBByLQO9I)). One breakdown explicitly frames this as a general technique: *"you can actually just sample drum sounds directly from songs that you like... if you can find like a clean kick sound or a clean clap or a clean hi-hat from a song that you like then you can just sample it and use it for your own productions."*
- "Kammy" snare programming: a "tight snappy rimshot" sample pitched up an octave for extra tightness, placed on every offbeat with a **two-16th-note snare roll** at the start of every two bars and a **three-note snare fill** at the end of the four-bar loop; **velocity variation is stressed repeatedly as "key for creating human energy."** 909 closed hats are filtered with a **12 dB/octave band-pass at ~4.8 kHz** ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/breaking-down-how-to-make-beats-inspired-by-fred-again/)).
- Shakers on "Kammy" are built from **three different one-shot cabasa samples** (not a chopped loop) — "Cabasa Filtered Chekov," "Cabasa Long Synth" (pitched down), "Cabasa Chelsea Short" — grouped together and balanced as a single instrument so hats and shakers "merge into one," while kick and snare remain the most prominent elements ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/breaking-down-how-to-make-beats-inspired-by-fred-again/)).

### Breakbeat/garage/two-step vs four-on-the-floor
- His drum vocabulary explicitly spans **UK garage/two-step, breakbeat/jungle, and four-on-the-floor house**, often within the same album or even the same track's different sections: "Kammy" is described as "two-step drums" (darker, minimal garage), while "Delilah" and "Marea" sit on more conventional 4/4 house kicks ([Scribd Actual Life 3 case study](https://www.scribd.com/document/959085711/Riad-Connor-Elbaba-Production-Styles-Written-Case-Study); [Jai Bhayani, Marea analysis](https://www.jaibhayani.com/post/how-we-ve-lost-dancing-ironically-got-the-world-dancing)).
- USB-era material leans further into breakbeat/jungle: one breakdown of his newer style explicitly calls for "some crazy drums as well, where we get a bit more of that breakbeat influence," reversing bounced audio and using **unquantized, "as natural as possible"** MIDI playing for the snare pattern to avoid a programmed feel — echoing his own "table drumming"/finger-drumming habits ([Why Everyone is Obsessed With Fred Again's Production](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XS33ea0wyOU)).
- **Rhythmic pockets / syncopation**: Fred reportedly uses the term "rhythmic pockets" regularly to describe deliberately accenting a **weak 16th-note step immediately before the kick**, rather than accenting the kick itself — this single-step syncopation move is cited as a core driver of his groove's danceability ([Underdog Music School, "The Fred Again.. phenomenon"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ByjKEl_uO9g)).

### Drum processing (saturation, transient shaping, glue)
- Consistent processing chain pattern across multiple remakes: subtractive EQ first (removing unwanted low end / harshness), then glue compression on the full drum bus (medium threshold, ~3:1 ratio) for cohesion, plus a dedicated **drum reverb aux** (e.g., "Chamber Smooth" algorithm, 100% wet on the send) so all drum layers share a consistent sense of space ([Mastering Fred Again's Production Techniques](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cr2c-Y5eLEY)).
- On "Kammy," the full drum bus runs through **Ableton Drum Buss** (compression, Soft Drive, Crunch, damping ~9.2 kHz) plus **Ableton Reverb** (Predelay 13.6 ms, Decay 1.4 s, input filter below 250 Hz removed, Diffusion network taming highs, light chorus, ~13% wet) — explicitly warns not to over-wet the drums so reverb doesn't "swamp" them ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/breaking-down-how-to-make-beats-inspired-by-fred-again/)).
- On "Rumble," heavier processing is used given the dubstep/grime context: **Decapitator** saturation on the kick/drum bus, **Oxford Inflator** for extra shine/intensity on the snare bus, plus dedicated clip (**OVC**) and limiting (**Pro-L2**) stages, and hats treated with heavy filtering, **AFX**, and a transient designer ([Freddie Fairweather remake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OOrnRYULQVM)).
- **Found-sound percussion**: MusicRadar documents Fred recording **finger/table taps on a smartphone mic** for lo-fi beat texture (referencing his Tiny Desk Concert), deliberately keeping an "accidental" quieter extra tap because it added feel, then tightening timing only slightly and copying the pattern to complete the bar rather than fully quantizing ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/the-best-feeling-is-when-you-play-back-the-thing-youve-made-that-day-and-you-actually-feel-good-about-it-thats-the-drug-im-chasing-every-day-unlocking-the-production-tricks-that-fred-again-swears-by)).

### Sidechain / pumping
- Sidechain compression from the kick is used across virtually every layer — piano, bass, pad — in the "Delilah" remake, each with its own threshold but similar 10 ms attack / 15 ms release settings feeding from a pre-effects kick signal, producing the "pumping" rhythmic breathing so associated with his sound (remake estimate) ([Jaron Lopez remake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4wkqzqMEYfQ)).
- A second, distinct sidechain technique noted by analysts: **"heavy sidechain with LFO Tool"** applied not to the source instrument but as an underwater/pulsing filter-and-volume effect on a muted/filtered element being slowly reintroduced — functionally similar to sidechaining but manually drawn/LFO-driven rather than triggered by a compressor ([Underdog Music School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ByjKEl_uO9g)).
- MusicRadar highlights a more advanced variant Fred is said to use: **sidechaining the reverb return itself** (rather than the dry source) using either a compressor or a tremolo plugin (panning removed, timing offset) to create a pumping effect specifically on the wet/ambient tail ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/the-best-feeling-is-when-you-play-back-the-thing-youve-made-that-day-and-you-actually-feel-good-about-it-thats-the-drug-im-chasing-every-day-unlocking-the-production-tricks-that-fred-again-swears-by)).

## 3. BASS MOVEMENT

### General role of bass in his mixes
- Bass generally **doubles or closely tracks the kick's rhythmic role** and is heavily sidechained to it, but the harmonic content (root motion of the chord progression) is carried by the bass rather than a separate instrument in several tracks (e.g., "Delilah") ([Jaron Lopez remake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4wkqzqMEYfQ)).
- "Rolling basslines" are called out repeatedly as a defining textural signature across *Actual Life 3*, used "to ground the compositions" underneath emotive pads/vocals ([Scribd case study](https://www.scribd.com/document/959085711/Riad-Connor-Elbaba-Production-Styles-Written-Case-Study)).

### Delilah (pull me out of this)
- Bassline plays the **roots of the chord progression** (E♭ → G → D) across two octaves with notes lengthened to eighth-note-triplet duration, giving a "rolling" swung feel rather than static quarter-note bass. Sound design: **Ableton Analog**, monophonic voice, Oscillator 1 square wave + Oscillator 2 saw wave (−4 dB, +2 octaves), filter cutoff 400 Hz / resonance 30%, filter envelope attack 150 ms / decay 595 ms / release 5 ms, small amount of sub-bass blended in via the Pedal panel (remake estimate) ([Jaron Lopez remake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4wkqzqMEYfQ)).
- Bass is sidechained to the kick (same chain as piano/pad, threshold around −32 dB) and EQ'd with a low cut at 50 Hz and a +2 dB boost at 1 kHz for presence in the mix (remake estimate) ([Jaron Lopez remake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4wkqzqMEYfQ)).

### Danielle (smile on my face)
- The bass is the track's most distinctive rhythmic-harmonic element: a **rolling triplet-grid pattern at 130 BPM**, four notes per measure (first note slightly longer for the "rolling" feel), moving from F–E in the first section and transposing down to C–B for measures 8–16 — i.e., arrangement variation achieved via bass transposition of the same rhythmic figure ([Alux Serum bass tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JPFaOFmZxYU)).
- Sound design (remake estimate): Serum saw wave, **MG Low 24 filter** with key-tracking on, cutoff ~107 Hz, filter envelope decay ~930 ms with sustain/release at 0 for a plucky-but-rolling shape, **mono voicing with portamento (glide) at 20%** to create the sliding note-to-note movement that defines the "rolling bass" feel, plus Hyper (unison/width), compression, light reverb (~4% size), and a peak EQ to control top end ([Alux Serum bass tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JPFaOFmZxYU)).

### Marea (we've lost dancing)
- Percussion and bass are kept minimal and unobtrusive, supporting rather than driving the arrangement — the four-on-the-floor kick and understated bass exist mainly "to support the vocal and harmonic elements without overpowering them" ([Jai Bhayani analysis](https://www.jaibhayani.com/post/how-we-ve-lost-dancing-ironically-got-the-world-dancing)).

### Rumble
- Bass is a **wave-folded Serum wobble bass**: base tone is an analog sine wave folded against a "Blue 12" wavetable shape via Serum's Warp/FM-from-B routing (oscillator B turned inaudible, used purely as an FM modulator), pitched an octave down for weight. Wobble motion comes from **LFO 3 set to a ramp shape at a quarter-note rate**, modulating wavetable position and filter cutoff simultaneously for the classic "bloop" dubstep wobble. Processing includes heavy compression, midrange scoop, and high-end reduction to sit the wobble low and wide in the mix (remake estimate; downloadable preset "Skrillex Killers.fxp" provided by the tutorial author) ([Freddie Fairweather remake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OOrnRYULQVM)).
- A separate **16th-note sub kick** functions almost like a rhythmic bass element (a kick "with saturation, cutting off the top end") layered against the wobble bass for additional low-end motion ([13 Fred Again Sounds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=irMBByLQO9I)).

### Turn On The Lights again..
- Bass includes a **sampled "bass drop" hit** (pitched up/transposed) layered with a custom Serum synth bass for the escalation moment, plus a distinct **wobble section** later in the track. Serum patch uses two Analog BD (basic-shapes) oscillators slightly detuned/out of phase for width, mono voicing with portamento on, MG Low 18 filter with drive, compressor, tube distortion, saturation, and EQ (roll off highs, boost ~225 Hz) (remake estimate) ([13 Fred Again Sounds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=irMBByLQO9I); [remake tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGb7mVJd0jU)). A pitch-bend is applied to the bass at the end of vocal phrases to add extra movement tied to the vocal phrasing ([remake tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGb7mVJd0jU)).

### General bass automation
- Multiple breakdowns note Fred **automating bass elements in and out via muting/filtering at transitions** (e.g., the "dropstep" — muting the first bass note at a loop's start) rather than writing new bass parts for each section, tying bass automation directly into his transition technique (see Section 5) ([Underdog Music School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ByjKEl_uO9g)).

## 4. VOCAL CHOPS / SAMPLING — his signature identity technique

### The core technique and its origin story
- Fred's entire *Actual Life* trilogy is built on a **"snatched field recordings" / "collaborative diary"** approach: sampling voice memos, Instagram clips, phone conversations, and spoken word from friends and strangers, then tuning/pitching/chopping the speech into melodic and rhythmic material. Each song on *Actual Life 3* is literally **named after the person whose voice or performance inspired it** (Delilah, Marea, Danielle, Kammy, Berwyn, Eyelar, Bleu, Nathan, Kelly, Mustafa, Clara, Winnie) ([Scribd Actual Life 3 case study](https://www.scribd.com/document/959085711/Riad-Connor-Elbaba-Production-Styles-Written-Case-Study)).
- Documented source-material examples:
  - **Delilah Montagu's** kitchen live performance of her own song "Lost Keys" → "Delilah (pull me out of this)" ([Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/fred-again/delilah-pull-me-out-of-this))
  - **The Blessed Madonna's** Zoom conversation about losing club culture during lockdown → "Marea (we've lost dancing)," entirely unwritten lyrics sampled from real speech ([Genius](https://genius.com/Fred-again-marea-weve-lost-dancing-lyrics); [dmy.co](https://dmy.co/new-music/the-blessed-madonna-fred-again-marea-weve-lost-dancing))
  - **070 Shake's (Danielle Balbuena)** own track "Nice to Have" → "Danielle (smile on my face)" ([Scribd case study](https://www.scribd.com/document/959085711/Riad-Connor-Elbaba-Production-Styles-Written-Case-Study))
  - **Obongjayar's** own ballad "I Wish It Was Me" retold as an uptempo garage-house track dedicated to siblings → "Adore u" ([Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/fred-again/adore-u); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adore_U))
  - **Minneapolis poet/rapper Guante (Kyle Tran Myhre)** performing at an open-mic night, sampled for "Berwyn (all that I got is you)" ([Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/fred-gibson-aka-fred-again?page=2))
  - An Instagram video of **Angie McMahon** playing a song on a bed, sampled for "Angie (I've Been Lost)" ([Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/fred-gibson-aka-fred-again?page=2))
  - A construction worker named **Carlos**, recorded on Fred's iPhone in an Atlanta bar, providing enthusiastic phrases used as source material ([Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/fred-gibson-aka-fred-again?page=2))
  - **Yung Bleu's** "You're Mines Still" sampled for "Bleu (Better With Time)"; **Kelly Zutrau/WET's** "Take Hold of Me" for "Kelly (End of a Nightmare)"; **Mustafa Ahmed's** Instagram performance of "Ali" for "Mustafa (Time To Move You)"; **Clara Ward and The Clara Ward Singers'** gospel recording "The Storm Is Passing Over" for "Clara (The Night Is Dark)" ([Scribd case study](https://www.scribd.com/document/959085711/Riad-Connor-Elbaba-Production-Styles-Written-Case-Study))
  - On the *USB* project: a Jamie T vocal reimagined for "Lightsburn Dimmer," and a sample from visual artist Splinter Frames built into "Solo," both manipulated heavily with Melodyne ([Tape Notes TN:176 transcript](https://podcasttranscript.ai/library/tn-176-fred-again))

### How he chops and pitches vocals — techniques
- **Tune the speech into melody**: on "Marea," Fred says directly, "I sampled it! And tuned her to be singing at times" — i.e., pitch-correcting spoken word onto a scale so conversational cadence becomes a sung hook ([Genius](https://genius.com/Fred-again-marea-weve-lost-dancing-lyrics)).
- **Slow-motion emotional reframing**: on "Delilah," Fred told Apple Music the concept behind manipulating tempo/pitch of a source clip: *"You know how sometimes if you see something in normal timing and then you see it in slow-mo, it's like, 'Oh, wow.' There's a whole new emotional framing for this."* ([Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/fred-again/delilah-pull-me-out-of-this))
- **Slice-to-MIDI / chop-and-play**: recordings are imported and divided into fragments, then mapped across pads (in Ableton via "Slice to MIDI," or via Maschine/Koala) so individual syllables/words can be triggered and rearranged rhythmically like a drum kit ([Gearnews step-by-step workflow](https://www.gearnews.com/fred-again-gear-sound-synths/)).
- **Drone + spoken word layering**: a documented technique for turning spoken-word poetry into melodic material — find a meaningful spoken-word clip (often searched directly on YouTube), keep a continuous ambient drone playing underneath while manipulating the vocal, use a looping/granular tool (e.g., **Paulstretch**) to stretch material infinitely, then run the vocal through **Melodyne** to pitch it into a melody and layer it back against the unprocessed spoken original for context ("Fred's production secrets," [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xwUTqc6gUQE)).
- **Vocal chop as lead synth**: multiple breakdowns identify a repeatable pattern — take one part of a vocal sample, chop into a rhythmic cell, run it through **pitch-shift and formant-shift plugins** (e.g., **Little Alter Boy**, or Logic's **Flex Pitch** shifting formants upward), then place these chops rhythmically as if it were a synth lead, sidechained to the kick via an LFO tool ducking effect ([How To Fred Again.. tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EmysxwjOZPA); [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/the-best-feeling-is-when-you-play-back-the-thing-youve-made-that-day-and-you-actually-feel-good-about-it-thats-the-drug-im-chasing-every-day-unlocking-the-production-tricks-that-fred-again-swears-by)).
- **Repetition of key words/phrases for hooks**: MusicRadar documents his method of isolating a 1–2 bar vocal section with strong rhythm or universal meaning, then finding a repeatable word (often the **last word of a phrase**) and looping/rearranging it using tools like Logic's **Beat Breaker**, **Cableguys TimeShaper 3**, or **Stutterizer (Repeat & Revealed)** to generate multiple rhythmic variations before picking the best one ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/the-best-feeling-is-when-you-play-back-the-thing-youve-made-that-day-and-you-actually-feel-good-about-it-thats-the-drug-im-chasing-every-day-unlocking-the-production-tricks-that-fred-again-swears-by)).
- **Reverse and pitch as arrangement variety tools**: an official Ableton-tutorial-adjacent writeup identifies using audio (not MIDI) so the chopped pattern is visible against the bar grid, then applying pitch-shift and sample-reverse as the two go-to manipulation moves for building a vocal-chop line ([Touch Loops](https://touchloops.com/blogs/news/how-to-sound-like-fred-again)).

### Vocal processing chain (confirmed by Fred + reverse-engineered)
- Confirmed by Fred himself: **reverb before compression** as the core vocal chain move (see Section 0), designed to exaggerate the "close voice / then room fills the gap" character of an iPhone recording ([Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/fred-gibson-aka-fred-again?page=2)).
- Reverse-engineered chains in remakes typically stack: multiband dynamics/compression (e.g., **OTT**-style), saturation, aggressive de-essing/EQ dips (commonly around 1 kHz to tame harshness), an eighth-note delay, and automated high-pass filtering at the end of vocal phrases to "filter off" the tail as it decays (remake estimate, "Turn On The Lights again.." breakdown) ([remake tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGb7mVJd0jU)).
- On "Delilah," the personal vocal-sample element is **detuned down an octave**, using Ableton's **Complex Pro** warp mode, then run through **Spectral Resonator** and **Hybrid Reverb** for a washed, ghostly texture distinct from the crisper lead vocal (remake estimate) ([Jaron Lopez remake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4wkqzqMEYfQ)).
- For extreme spectral manipulation, Fred and mentor Brian Eno have used **Expert Sleepers' Spectral Conquest** — a spectral EQ capable of surgically removing individual frequencies or completely reworking a full song/vocal into new granular-synthesis-like material ([Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/fred-gibson-aka-fred-again?page=2)).

### Why this defines his identity
- Analysts frame this explicitly as the mechanism of his emotional impact, not just a gimmick: *"The signature approach... is the use of found audio as compositional material... [Fred] understands how to find the pitch in a spoken voice, how to loop it at the right moment, how to let the grain of the recording, the ambient noise, the imperfection, become part of the texture rather than something to be cleaned away. This is closer to the tradition of Steve Reich and tape music than it is to conventional DJ production."* ([The DJ Diaries](https://thedj-diaries.com/fred-again-electronic-music-what-he-did/))
- The live show visually reinforces the technique: a large screen displays clips from the phones/social media of the people sampled, which Fred calls "part of the DNA of the music" ([Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/fred-again-wows-zane-lowe-mpc-new-interview-1234618577/)).

## 5. TRANSITIONS

### The "dropstep" technique (named informally by analysts, observed across many tracks)
- At the very start of a loop/section, Fred **mutes almost everything** (bass, key rhythmic elements, sometimes hats) for a brief instant, leaving only the kick, then adds a small vocal stab and/or **reversed white noise** before the full loop resumes. This creates a "falling into the loop" sensation and functions as a musical "marker" for where the loop begins, rather than a hard, fully-arranged arrival ([Underdog Music School, "The Fred Again.. phenomenon"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ByjKEl_uO9g)). Practically: automate a Utility gain device to **−∞ dB** on the muted elements for a single beat/16th note, keep only the first kick, layer in the vocal stab + reversed noise.
- This is described as a recurring, near-signature move — "Fred again uses this technique quite regularly" — rather than a one-off effect.

### Filter movement as the primary build/release tool
- Rather than only fading volume, Fred frequently **hides an element behind a low-pass filter, then automates the filter open gradually** to bring it "out from underwater" into the mix, later closing it again to remove the element without a hard cut. This is described as giving listeners time to acclimatize to a complex part's rhythm before it's fully revealed, versus dropping it in from silence, which can feel like "too big" an event ([Underdog Music School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ByjKEl_uO9g)).
- Paired with **heavy LFO Tool modulation** on the same filtered element to add a pulsing, underwater-like sidechain-adjacent movement distinct from standard kick-triggered sidechain ([Underdog Music School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ByjKEl_uO9g)).
- On "Turn On The Lights again..," a comparable device is used at the end of vocal phrases: an **EQ8 automated to progressively cut low end**, filtering the tail of the phrase up into just high frequencies as it fades — a "filtering off into the air" transition move (remake estimate) ([remake tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGb7mVJd0jU)).

### Build-ups, risers, and silence/space
- One full-track recreation documents a concrete pre-drop transition: **filter out everything just before the drop, leave a genuinely silent 1–2 second gap**, then hit a drum fill plus the key vocal line simultaneously, creating "that very stark contrast" pulling the listener into the drop. On the first beat of the resulting drop, **almost nothing else plays except a kick and a delayed vocal** — deliberately holding back full-arrangement impact for a beat or two to create a "sucking" sensation into the drop rather than an immediate wall of sound ("How to Fred Again (Step by Step)," [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YXKwsGA3fJI)).
- The same breakdown documents a "washed out," distant intro treatment inspired directly by "Delilah": short reverb + high cut + heavy stereo widening on the master bus, all gradually automated to decrease/narrow across the intro, simulating the sound of "the track being played from another room," then gradually clarifying as if physically walking into the space ([same source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YXKwsGA3fJI)).
- Between drop sections, small connective devices are used to "fill space" rather than leaving true silence — e.g., a short pitch-swept sine wave run bridging two halves of a drop, or a warped/stretched vocal texture created by putting a sample on Warp mode "Texture," holding Shift, and manually stretching it for a sustained, sighing transition sound ([same source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YXKwsGA3fJI)).

### Sidechain and reverb automation as transition glue
- MusicRadar frames automation of **reverb send levels** (not just static wet amounts) as central to how his transitions breathe: applying reverb to specific words at the end of vocal phrases so the tail "fills the space" afterward, and/or automating a reverb swell into a transition point for added lift ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/the-best-feeling-is-when-you-play-back-the-thing-youve-made-that-day-and-you-actually-feel-good-about-it-thats-the-drug-im-chasing-every-day-unlocking-the-production-tricks-that-fred-again-swears-by)).
- Sidechaining the **reverb return itself** (rather than the dry signal) with a compressor or tremolo plugin, automated in depth, is highlighted as a distinctive way he keeps big ambient tails from clouding transition points while still using them for drama ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/the-best-feeling-is-when-you-play-back-the-thing-youve-made-that-day-and-you-actually-feel-good-about-it-thats-the-drug-im-chasing-every-day-unlocking-the-production-tricks-that-fred-again-swears-by)).

### Marea and Delilah breakdown transitions specifically
- "Marea"'s breakdown section is placed atypically — a full stripped-back moment mid-track (not just pre-drop) foregrounding vocal + pads only, explicitly built to create "a moment of introspection before the energy builds back up" ([Jai Bhayani analysis](https://www.jaibhayani.com/post/how-we-ve-lost-dancing-ironically-got-the-world-dancing)).
- "Delilah"'s drum pattern "briefly [takes] respite midway through before restoring the beat," mirroring the anxiety/panic-attack narrative structurally (drop in energy = the panic peak, restoration = being "pulled out") ([The Indiependent](https://www.indiependent.co.uk/track-review-delilah-pull-me-out-of-this-fred-again/)).

## 6. EMOTIONAL PACING

### Dynamic range and contrast as narrative device
- Analysts consistently describe his catalogue-wide approach as **contrasting club-oriented propulsion against sparse, vulnerable arrangements** within the same body of work (and sometimes within the same track): fast/euphoric/punchy ("Delilah") vs. dark/minimal/brooding ("Kammy") vs. slow/piano-led/whispered/intimate ("Mustafa") vs. stripped-back/ambient/fragile ("Berwyn") — a deliberate dynamic-range strategy across the album rather than a single formula ([Scribd Actual Life 3 case study](https://www.scribd.com/document/959085711/Riad-Connor-Elbaba-Production-Styles-Written-Case-Study)).
- On "Marea," the dynamic range is "carefully controlled, with subtle automation of volume and effects to maintain interest and drive throughout the track," and the mid-track breakdown is specifically engineered as an emotional "moment of introspection before the energy builds back up" ([Jai Bhayani analysis](https://www.jaibhayani.com/post/how-we-ve-lost-dancing-ironically-got-the-world-dancing)).
- On "Delilah," the drum pattern's brief mid-song "respite" is read directly as mirroring "his thumping heart, swollen with anxiety as he grapples with escaping the crowd" — i.e., arrangement dynamics are consciously mapped onto the emotional content of the (panic attack) lyric ([The Indiependent](https://www.indiependent.co.uk/track-review-delilah-pull-me-out-of-this-fred-again/)).

### Found audio and spoken word as emotional anchoring
- The core emotional-pacing device across his work is **using real, unscripted speech as the emotional "truth" of a track**, then building dance-music structure around it rather than writing lyrics to fit a beat. On "Marea": *"He didn't write lyrics, he didn't craft a hook in a studio session. He just listened to his friend speak from the heart, and turned it into something the whole world could feel... it connected in a way that very few records do, because it wasn't performed, it was real."* ([Instagram reel summary of the Marea story](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DWETzEJCOnX/))
- A close reading of "Delilah" frames the emotional arc explicitly as narrative tension engineered into the mix: *"it spirals (like a panic thought) and then it lifts (like your friends showing up)... you're not waiting for a beat - you're waiting for a turning point."* ([The Internet Digest / Kashvi](https://kashvi.substack.com/p/why-everyone-loves-fred-again))
- Analysts tie the technical grain of the sampling directly to emotional authenticity: *"[he understands] how to let the grain of the recording, the ambient noise, the imperfection, become part of the texture rather than something to be cleaned away"* — i.e., deliberately preserving lo-fi artifacts (phone compression, room tone, background noise) is treated as an emotional-pacing tool, not a flaw to be mixed out ([The DJ Diaries](https://thedj-diaries.com/fred-again-electronic-music-what-he-did/)).

### Tension/release mechanics used for emotional effect
- The **drone technique** (single sustained note/chord, run through maximal reverb, held under an entire section or track) is used to create ambient emotional context before rhythmic or melodic elements enter — a direct carry-over from his time with Brian Eno, who reportedly spent "hours" with Fred just listening to how a single drone evolves ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/fred-again-zane-lowe-native-instruments-maschine-live-set/); ["Fred's production secrets," YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xwUTqc6gUQE)).
- **Scale-degree choice for drones/harmony is treated as an emotional dial**: analysts citing his own commentary note that the **5th degree** of the scale is the "sweetest"/most consonant choice for a drone, while the **3rd degree** introduces more tension and ambiguity — i.e., he consciously selects scale degrees to control the emotional temperature of a passage ([Underdog Music School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ByjKEl_uO9g)).
- Harmonic "spice" as an emotional tool: layering a **major seventh-type voicing on the iii chord** (playing the V chord over the iii root) after a run of "safe" IV–VI chords is described as adding sudden richness/complexity/jazz color at a key emotional moment, contrasted with the simpler triads used elsewhere — deliberately alternating harmonic simplicity and complexity for pacing ([Underdog Music School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ByjKEl_uO9g)).
- **Vocal manipulation for intimacy vs. spectacle**: stutters, blends, and layering are used to create "textural intimacy," while the same toolkit (chopping, pitching, repetition) is used elsewhere to build euphoric, anthemic hooks — the emotional register is set by *how* the same chop techniques are applied (soft/whispered/close-mic'd vs. hard/rhythmic/pitched-up) ([Scribd Actual Life 3 case study](https://www.scribd.com/document/959085711/Riad-Connor-Elbaba-Production-Styles-Written-Case-Study)).
- Reverb-before-compression on vocals (Section 0/4) is itself an emotional-pacing tool in Fred's own words: it "hyper-emphasizes the singer's acoustic space" and "gives the vocal an emotional context" — the room tone becomes a stand-in for atmosphere/isolation/vulnerability ([Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/fred-gibson-aka-fred-again?page=2)).

### Meta-level emotional intent (in his own words)
- On his broader artistic aim, discussing the *Actual Life* project's fleeting-moment source material: *"I think the feeling that I became really obsessed with was like trying to take the very fleeting moments and trying to expose as much beauty as is in them."* ([We Rave You, Zane Lowe interview coverage](https://weraveyou.com/2022/10/fred-again-zane-lowe-apple-music-interview/))
- Reviewers of live shows describe the resulting audience experience directly in emotional-arc terms — a KEXP piece on his concerts is titled *"Navigating Grief and Love at a Fred again.. Concert,"* underscoring that his pacing choices (buildup, breakdown, release) are read by audiences as genuine emotional narrative, not just DJ-set dynamics ([KEXP](https://kexp.org/read/2023/2/3/the-most-human-you-can-be-navigating-grief-and-love-at-a-fred-again-concert/)).

## Quick-Reference: Practice Exercises Derived From This Research

1. **Drone-first writing**: Build a single sustained note/chord through a maximal-length reverb (10-sec decay, 100% late reflections) as your harmonic bed before writing any rhythm. Choose the 3rd degree of the scale for tension, the 5th for consonance.
2. **Vocal-as-drum-kit**: Take a 1–2 bar spoken-word or conversational clip, slice it to MIDI/pads, and try triggering syllables rhythmically against a kick before you decide on a "melody."
3. **Reverb-before-compressor vocal chain**: On any vocal, insert reverb *before* your compressor instead of after, and A/B against the standard order.
4. **Dropstep transition**: At a loop's start, automate a Utility gain to −∞ dB on bass + one rhythmic element for exactly one beat, leave only the kick, add a vocal stab + reversed white noise, then release into the full loop.
5. **Rhythmic pocket**: In a 16-step loop, instead of accenting the kick step, accent the 16th-note step immediately *before* it and see how it changes the "wanting to dance" feel.
6. **Filter-reveal instead of volume-fade**: Introduce a complex synth/vocal part by opening a low-pass filter gradually (with heavy LFO Tool modulation) instead of simply raising its fader from silence.
7. **Found-sound percussion**: Record a tap/knock on your phone mic, keep an "accidental" quieter secondary hit if it happens, and build a kick pattern from just two performed hits copied around the bar rather than one perfectly quantized sample.
8. **Plugin minimalism discipline**: For one week, use only one EQ, one compressor, and one reverb plugin across every session — per Fred's own stated advice — and measure whether song output increases.
